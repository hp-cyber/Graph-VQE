Scheduling and host safety (2026-10-27; same as the VQE_Docking launchers)

run.sh uses GRAPH_VQE_GPUS (this server: 2,3,4,5,6) or else every GPU; --gpus overrides. There is no fixed
number of jobs per GPU and no per-job RAM/GPU budget. tools/admission.py starts one
job at a time (at most one every 30 s across every launcher sharing
ANALYTIC_MPS_ADMISSION_DIR) while the host stays responsive, on the GPU with the
fewest jobs whose CPU socket is below 95% busy over 10 s and that has at least 10%
of its memory free (nvidia-smi, queried when a start is due; ANALYTIC_MPS_GPU_MIN_FREE
changes the fraction). If no GPU qualifies, or nvidia-smi fails or takes over 30 s,
no job starts that round. Every 2 s a watcher checks:
  - a normal-priority fork/exec + fsync probe finishes within 2 s;
  - memory and I/O "full" pressure (PSI avg10) stay at or below 10% and 50%
    (read as 0 where the kernel has no /proc/pressure);
  - available RAM stays at or above 5%.
After 3 failed checks the newest job is stopped (SIGTERM, SIGKILL after 10 s) and
requeued, and no job starts until another finishes. At 90% RAM use every job is
stopped and requeued; starts wait until RAM falls. RAM is the host's, or the
container's cgroup limit when lower. Stopped jobs resume from their checkpoints.
A job that exits with a GPU out-of-memory message in its run.log output (CUDA,
cuBLAS/cuSOLVER/cuTensorNet allocation failures, PyTorch, or the graph/reverse-mode
GPU preflight) while other jobs share its GPU is requeued at the end of the queue and
resumes from its checkpoint (campaign_status.json: oom_requeued). Alone on its GPU,
it is recorded as failed: the job itself does not fit.
Files force_unresponsive and force_ram_full in the admission directory trigger
these by hand.

Each worker runs in its own session at nice 10 and lowest best-effort I/O priority,
pinned to the CPU socket next to its GPU (all allowed CPUs where that is unknown),
with no BLAS/OpenMP thread limit. It is killed if the launcher dies; a new
launcher first kills leftover workers of this campaign. On exit, the process group
is killed and RAM and CUDA context release are confirmed (at most 60 s each) in a
background thread. memory_usage.json records sampled peak RAM/VRAM (VRAM sampled
once a minute) and the release result; evicted attempts are not recorded there.
Ctrl+C, SIGTERM or a closed terminal stop every worker (SIGTERM, SIGKILL after 60 s).

Independent graph strategy (updated global ansatz)
Both local and global circuits now use four-layer nearest-neighbor linear or
reverse_linear SU2. The cap is min(2**4, 2**floor(width/2)): at most 16, or 8
at width 7. No additional graph-edge gates or noncontiguous block circuits
are appended to the global ansatz. All Hamiltonian terms remain in its energy.
The local best parameters initialize global rotations by qubit/layer/gate.

resource_preflight.json predicts allocations for the actual nearest-neighbor
circuit and its zero-cutoff SVD cap before optimization. Reverse gradients now
use the bounded reverse-MPS path. The previous 2,147,483,647 global cap and
oversized 97-qubit graph-circuit estimates no longer describe this ansatz.
Global accuracy here means exact simulation of this new ansatz (within floating
point precision), not preservation of the former graph-gate variational family.

All local campaign results were cleared for a fresh run, including completed
cases and old validation outputs. New validation uses separate directories;
production runs/ remains empty. The full campaign has not been restarted.

Fresh incomplete cases on another server
Stop that campaign, then run from the Graph-VQE repository:
  python3 test/tools/clean_unfinished.py test/runs --apply
Without --apply, it reports what would be removed. Completed and locked/running
cases are preserved. Inputs and lock files remain; all incomplete checkpoints,
logs and outputs are removed. Use a new output directory when switching from the old graph-gate ansatz;
its checkpoints are incompatible with the new global parameter layout.

Deploy the modified test directory to the other server. Its installed CUDA-Q
plugin also needs the preceding stable-SVD repair; use the updated
test/setup_local_cudaq.sh there. Changing this local checkout cannot update
another server's installed binary automatically.
